# Sports injury storytelling

Read `content_system/guides/sports_injury_storytelling.md` and `content_system/guides/fact_check_protocol.md` first.

## The three mandatory elements (an episode isn't finished without all three)
1. **A story** grounded in verified facts: who, the stakes, the turn, the consequence.
2. **The injury explained:** the structure, what it does, the known mechanism, why it matters for an athlete, what it does and doesn't imply about recovery. Tag these lines `[SCI]`. **At least 2 `[SCI]` lines, and the first one in the first half** of the script (regression-checked). Science only at the end is an add-on.
3. **A lesson for the viewer** that the story earns: return to sport vs training, progressive loading, timelines as estimates, criteria over the calendar, recognise-and-remove, and so on. The ending ties the story and the lesson together.

## Steps
1. **Case selection:** a player with a documented injury (club/federation statement, reputable reporting) and a real connection to African football/AFCON. Reject cases whose diagnosis is unknown, or label them as a bonus with the limitation stated.
2. **Case sheet:** identity · the injury as documented · date and circumstances · public treatment/return facts · what is NOT known · general science for that injury (with sources) · the lesson.
3. **Separate documented from general.** Player facts are stated as facts with attribution. Physiology is general («الرباط الصليبي… غالبًا»), and NOTES say it isn't his private treatment.
4. **Choose a structure** that differs from the other episodes (calendar race, delayed reveal, paradox, anatomy-led, an estimate that stretches, a bookend…).
5. Draft → lint → second pass to the 60–80 s budget → re-check facts on the final wording.

## Never
Invent diagnoses, mechanisms, surgeries, rehab exercises, setbacks, return dates, quotes, feelings or motives. Never claim a movement caused an injury without evidence. Never turn a lucky outcome into the lesson (see S3, Mané).
