# Fact-check protocol (science and sports history)

## 1. Claim ledger
List every factual claim in the draft, spoken or on screen, with a label:

| Label | Meaning | How it may be said |
|---|---|---|
| VERIFIED | checked against a primary source (an abstract at least), or an official record / reputable match report, in this session | plainly, with context |
| MECHANISM | biomechanical/physiological support, not proof of outcome | «غالبًا»، «من أخطر اللحظات» |
| PRINCIPLE | an established training principle without one trial behind the wording | as coaching logic |
| CARRIED | taken from an earlier research file with its sources, not re-verified | only as the source states it; keep its «ممنوع يتقال» list |
| CONFLICTING | sources disagree (minutes, totals) | a range or rounding («قبل النهاية بخمس دقايق») |
| UNSUPPORTED | can't be supported | cut it, or rewrite around it |

## 2. Science
- PubMed MCP (`search_articles` → `get_article_metadata`), or the journal/full text. Check n, population, comparison, outcome and follow-up.
- Prefer absolute numbers (33% → 22%) to relative ones (35% less).
- Put n on screen when it's small. Don't move elite-athlete results to everyone without a qualifier.
- No fear framing about pain, imaging or posture. No absolutes beyond the evidence.

## 3. Sports history
- Results, scorers, minutes, dates: the official competition record or a reference compilation (RSSSF, the tournament Wikipedia page with its citations), plus a contemporary match report for *how* something happened.
- **Reported speech:** paraphrase, attribute, and give the year («حكى في 2017»). Never put invented words in a player's mouth.
- **Motives and feelings:** only if the person stated them. «جه يعيش إحساس البطل» is OK because he said it. «كان حزين» is not OK unless it's documented.
- **Rivalries:** state results; don't editorialise («انتقام»). Note any context that changes how a score should be read (red cards, a suspended referee).
- Record discrepancies in NOTES and resolve them with ranges.

## 4. Never
Fabricate a citation, DOI, quote, number, minute or scene. If you didn't verify it, say so in NOTES.
