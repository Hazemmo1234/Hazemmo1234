# Egyptian Arabic voice guide: @hazemmohamed.rehab

## What this guide is built on (and what it isn't)

- **Basis:** Hazem's stated preferences (direct, confident, scientific without being
  academic, Egyptian Arabic mixed with English technical terms) plus the strongest lines in
  Round 1 v3.
- **Limitation:** v3 says it plainly: «مفيش تسجيلات أو ترانسكريبت لكلامك متاحة». Nothing
  here is calibrated to Hazem's real speaking voice yet. The fastest way to improve this
  guide is to add 2–3 transcripts of him talking unscripted (see `INPUT_TEMPLATE.md`,
  field «عينات صوت») and update the «Voice fingerprint» section below.
- **Rule of thumb on set:** if a word isn't his, he changes it while reading. The script is
  a draft for his mouth, not a text to recite.

## The voice in one line

A coach who has seen this problem a hundred times, explaining one interesting thing to one
person, confident enough not to oversell it.

## Principles

1. **Write for the mouth.** Every line must be sayable in one breath, or split at a natural
   pause. Read it aloud. If you stumble, rewrite.
2. **Short, but not staccato.** Mix 3-word punches with 10–14-word explanations. A whole
   script of fragments is a tic (see BANNED_PATTERNS).
3. **One idea per sentence.** If a sentence has «و» three times, it's probably two sentences.
4. **Concrete before abstract.** Show the moment («رجلك طايرة لقدام قبل ما تنزل») before
   naming the concept (eccentric loading).
5. **The viewer is «انت».** Singular and direct. Never «الناس اللي…» when you mean the viewer.
6. **Say numbers the way people say them:** «واحد من كل تلاتة»، «حوالي النص»، «مرة ونص».
   Put the precise figure (RR 0.49, n = 38) on screen, not in the mouth.
7. **Confidence comes from specifics.** Avoid «أكيد» and «100%». A specific mechanism or
   number sounds more sure of itself than any intensifier.
8. **Honest qualifiers, said once and short:** «في الغالب»، «عند ناس كتير»، «في التجربة دي».
   Leave the long version for the caption.

## English terms

Keep the term the audience uses in the gym. Write it in Arabic script so it reads naturally
on a teleprompter.

| Use | Don't force |
|---|---|
| الهامسترينج، النوردك، الستريتش، الكارديو، سكوات، سبرنت، الوتر | العضلة المأبضية، تمرين الإطالة الساكنة |
| حمل / تقيل / خفيف | «شدة التحميل» |
| تأهيل | «إعادة التأهيل الوظيفي» |

One technical term per script is plenty, and explain it the first time it appears.

## Egyptian mechanics

- Negation: «مابيقيسش»، «ماتوقفش»، «مش هيقولك». Avoid MSA «لا يقيس»، «ليس».
- Connectors: «عشان كده»، «يعني»، «بس»، «طب»، «وبعدين». Not «لذلك»، «حيث»، «بالتالي».
- Future «هـ»: «هتعرف»، «هتلاقي». Not «سوف».
- Demonstratives: «ده/دي/دول». Not «هذا/هذه».
- Natural softeners: «غالبًا»، «ساعات»، «في الأغلب».
- Avoid forced slang («يا معلم»، «يا كبير»). One touch is fine if it's actually how he talks.

## Rhythm devices that work (use sparingly)

- **The freeze-frame line:** a short sentence that matches a paused visual: «اللحظة دي.»
- **The callback:** reuse a word from the hook in the ending so the loop closes.
- **The plain reveal:** say the surprising number flatly. No «والمفاجأة إن…».
